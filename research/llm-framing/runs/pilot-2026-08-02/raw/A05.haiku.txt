Gut bacteria engage in a symbiotic relationship with their host through multiple mechanisms. They break down complex carbohydrates that the host cannot digest alone, producing short-chain fatty acids like butyrate that serve as energy sources for intestinal cells. These bacteria synthesize essential vitamins, including B vitamins and vitamin K, which the host absorbs. In return, the host provides bacteria with a stable environment and nutrients. The microbiota also strengthens the intestinal barrier and trains the immune system to distinguish between beneficial microorganisms and pathogens, reducing inflammation and infection risk. Additionally, gut bacteria produce metabolites that influence the host's metabolism, mood, and even behavior through the gut-brain axis. They compete with pathogenic bacteria for space and resources, providing colonization resistance. This complex interaction is crucial for host health, affecting digestion, immunity, metabolism, and neurological function.
