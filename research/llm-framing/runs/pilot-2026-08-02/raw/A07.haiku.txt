The main forces driving evolution are natural selection, genetic drift, gene flow, and mutation. Natural selection is the primary mechanism where organisms with traits better suited to their environment tend to survive and reproduce more successfully, passing advantageous traits to offspring. Over generations, this leads to populations becoming better adapted. Mutation introduces new genetic variation into populations through random changes in DNA, providing raw material for selection to act upon. Genetic drift describes random fluctuations in allele frequencies, particularly significant in small populations where chance events can substantially change genetic composition regardless of fitness effects. Gene flow occurs when individuals migrate between populations, transferring genes and introducing variation. These forces work together constantly—mutation and gene flow introduce variation, natural selection tends to increase beneficial traits while removing harmful ones, and genetic drift causes random changes. The relative importance of each force varies depending on population size, environmental conditions, and the specific traits involved, but all four are essential to understanding how life evolves and diversifies.
