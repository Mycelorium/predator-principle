Long-run productivity comes from accumulating the things that let a given amount of labour produce more: physical capital, human capital, and above all useful knowledge. Technology is the only input that doesn't run into diminishing returns, so sustained growth depends on a society's capacity to generate ideas and, just as importantly, to adopt and diffuse them across ordinary firms rather than a handful of frontier ones. That capacity rests on institutions — secure property rights, contract enforcement, competitive markets that let productive firms grow and unproductive ones die, and openness to trade and migration, which widen both the market and the talent pool. Deep capital markets channel savings toward risky, high-return projects. Education and health determine how much of the population can participate. Stable macroeconomic policy and predictable rules lower the discount rate on long-horizon investment. Geography, energy costs, and infrastructure matter at the margin. What consistently fails is protecting incumbents, since that trades measured stability for stagnation.
