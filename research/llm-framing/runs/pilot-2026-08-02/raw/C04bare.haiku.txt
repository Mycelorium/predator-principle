Long-term economic productivity depends on several reinforcing factors. Capital accumulation—physical infrastructure, machinery, and financial resources—enables production at scale. Human capital through education and skills development makes workers more productive and capable of innovation. Technological progress, driven by research and competitive markets, increases efficiency and creates new opportunities. Institutional quality matters critically: strong property rights, rule of law, and low corruption encourage investment and entrepreneurship. Macroeconomic stability, including reasonable inflation and sustainable debt levels, provides predictability for long-term planning. Open trade and competitive markets incentivize efficiency and expose firms to best practices globally. Savings and investment rates determine how much capital is available for productive activities. Finally, social cohesion and political stability reduce uncertainty and transaction costs. These factors compound over time—better institutions attract investment, which builds infrastructure, which enables productivity gains that generate wealth for further investment, creating virtuous cycles that distinguish persistently prosperous economies from stagnating ones.
